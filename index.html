EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Know your core customer.

The dataset indicates a dominant female customer base with higher engagement and spending potential, particularly among married women without children.

KEY INSIGHT

Female customers account for the majority of restaurant visits and represent the primary revenue-driving segment.

Operational and strategic implications

Operational Insight: Satisfaction is generally positive, but a notable dissatisfaction cluster suggests retention risk among core customers.

Strategic Impact: Improving food quality and targeting premium experiences can significantly increase repeat visitation and revenue.

These are interpretations from the original project; the page does not supply supporting revenue figures or an estimated effect size.

RESEARCH APPROACH

Consumer perspectives, with context.

This portfolio presents existing consumer survey analysis across gender, visits, income, and satisfaction to inform restaurant marketing and customer experience strategies.

Evidence and limitations

The original project is a single executive analysis page. No underlying dataset, questionnaire, sampling method, survey dates, sample count, bibliography, or external references were supplied.

The requested “500+ responses” cannot be verified from the existing repository and is therefore not presented as a finding. The 72% female share, 28% male share, and 64% Retention Index are reported exactly as in the source. The source does not define the Retention Index; it should not be interpreted as a measured repeat-visit rate.

Income and satisfaction charts retain the original relative bar widths. Those widths are not labeled percentages in the source, have no documented units, and do not form a verified distribution. They are shown as illustrative source scales, not new quantitative findings.

The source reports a 64% Retention Index and identifies dissatisfaction as a retention risk. Its calculation and relationship to actual repeat visitation are not documented.

Explore the business implication

Improve food consistency and presentation to reduce dissatisfaction. The original research points to a cluster of dissatisfied core customers; the supplied page does not quantify its size or prove causation.
