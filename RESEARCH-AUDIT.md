## Superseded earlier website claims

The previous page reported 72% female share, 28% male share, and an undefined
64% Retention Index. The report’s tables supersede those demographic
figures; the undefined index has been removed rather than recalculated.
“500+ responses” is replaced by exactly 500. The illustrative income bar widths
have been replaced by page 3 record counts with clearly labeled denominators. The married-female/no-children subgroup is supported by Question 1A. Earlier
claims of total trips, greater spending, or generally positive satisfaction
are not inferred from record counts or the nearly even food-rating distribution.

## Additional verified tables and source limitations

- Page 1: marital/parental record counts total 500 (female 275, male 225).
  Married female records total 101: 58 without children and 43 with children.
  Female parental totals are 144/131; male totals are 117/108 (no/yes children).
- Page 3: household-income totals are 93, 87, 87, 77, 52, 52, 52 in ascending
  income-category order. Female counts: 40,34,34,44,40,41,42; male counts:
  53,53,53,33,12,11,10. Counts total 500. Percentages divide each total by 500.
- Page 6: negative food ratings include 113 female and 86 male respondents.
- Page 7 groups the 113 female records into current-year visit-frequency bins.
  Its narrative calls 113 a visit total, but the displayed pivot counts records.
  The site explicitly distinguishes these and does not invent a sum of trips.
- The page 1/page 3 pivots are Count of #VisitsPriorYear/Count of
  #VisitsCurrentYear, not sums. Their figures are shown as record counts.
- The question about recommending the restaurant is not accompanied by a
  recommendation yes/no breakdown; no recommendation or retention rate is
  inferred from the 199 negative-rating records.
